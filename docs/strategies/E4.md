# E4 — IPO listing gains

Status: DRAFT. Locks at its first development run. Judged by `GATE.md` on its
own capital sleeve (§8), not on E1's portfolio.

## 1. Purpose
Test, across every mainboard IPO since 2010, whether a rule for applying to
IPOs and selling on listing day earns more than the same capital in the
liquid sleeve, after allotment odds, lock-in, costs and tax. Grey-market
premium (GMP) is tested as a sweep row from 2019, when its history begins;
the main rule uses exchange data only so the whole history is usable.

## 2. Hypothesis
Listing-day returns of oversubscribed mainboard IPOs are positive on average;
the binding constraint is allotment probability, not signal. The expected
value per application is positive but small, and it is uncorrelated with E1.

## 3. Rule (main)
For each mainboard IPO: apply for one retail lot if the **last subscription
figure published before the retail cutoff on the final day** shows total
subscription ≥ 5×. Sell the allotted shares at the listing-day close.
No SME IPOs. One application (one PAN) per issue.

## 4. Allotment model (deterministic, declared)
- Retail subscription ≤ 1× → full allotment of the lot.
- Retail subscription r > 1× → probability of allotment p = 1/r (lottery of
  one lot per successful applicant, SEBI's retail allotment rule).
- Expected value per issue = p × (listing close − issue price) × lot size,
  minus costs, minus tax, minus cash cost of the blocked amount for the
  lock-in days (ASBA block from application to listing: T+6 before
  December 2023, T+3 after).
Reported both as expected value (deterministic) and as 1,000 Monte Carlo
draws of actual allotment (for the variance).

## 5. Data
- IPO list: NSE past issues (issue price, lot size, dates, category-wise
  subscription at close) from 2010. Chittorgarh as a cross-check.
- Listing-day open/close from the panel (first bhavcopy row of the ISIN).
- Subscription snapshots: exchange intraday figures where archived; else the
  final-day close figure with the lookahead declared and S1 as the check.
- GMP: scraped history from 2019 (IPO Watch / Investorgain archives); each
  value must carry the date it was captured, else it is dropped.

## 6. Window
Development 2010–2016. Validation 2017–2021. Sealed 2022–2025 (the busiest
IPO years; untouched).

## 7. Sweep
| row | change |
|---|---|
| S1 | subscription figure from day 2 close (removes any final-day lookahead) |
| S2 | threshold 3× |
| S3 | threshold 10× |
| S4 | retail-category subscription instead of total |
| S5 | sell at listing-day open |
| S6 | hold 5 sessions |
| S7 | GMP ≥ 15% of issue price on the last bidding day (2019+ only) |
| S8 | apply to every mainboard IPO (no threshold) — the no-skill baseline |

## 8. Success (replaces G1–G2 for this experiment; G3–G4 apply)
In both development and validation:
- after-tax return on average blocked capital ≥ cash rate + 5 pp/yr
- positive after-tax P&L in at least 70% of calendar years
- expected annual after-tax P&L per PAN reported in rupees (this is the
  number that decides whether it is worth doing)
Scaling table reported for 1, 2, 4 PANs (family applications are outside the
system; the table shows what they would be worth).

## 9. Capital
Separate sleeve: ₹2 lakh, never mixed with E1's cash. Blocked amounts come
out of this sleeve; the rest sits at the cash rate.

## 10. Not claimed
No SME issues. No secondary-market IPO trading after listing day. No claim
about GMP's reliability beyond the S7 row.
