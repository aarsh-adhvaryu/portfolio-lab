# E3 — Defensive sleeve (asset-class trend during RISK_OFF)

Status: DRAFT. Locks at its first development run. Judged by `GATE.md`.

## 1. Purpose
E1's RISK_OFF sends new cash to a flat 6% sleeve. Test whether letting that
cash follow the strongest trend among a small set of ETFs improves after-tax
return or drawdown. This is the "opportunistic in a crash" goal in a
testable form.

## 2. Hypothesis
During equity RISK_OFF, gold (in rupees) or a broad equity ETF is often in
its own uptrend; a trend-selected sleeve beats cash over the RISK_OFF months
without adding equity-like drawdown.

## 3. Sleeve candidates
- Liquid ETF (LIQUIDBEES, listed 2003) — the cash proxy
- Gold ETF (GOLDBEES, listed 2007)
- Nifty 50 ETF (NIFTYBEES, listed 2002)
Silver ETF excluded (listed 2022, no history). Candidates are identified by
ISIN prefix `INF` and instrument_type ETF in the panel.

## 4. Rule (the one change)
At a RISK_OFF month-end M: compute each candidate's 10-month return and
10-month average price. Investable cash (new SIP plus any accumulated
sleeve) goes to the candidate with the highest 10-month return among those
trading above their 10-month average; if none, the liquid ETF. At the first
RISK_ON month-end, the sleeve is sold at the next open and cash re-enters
through E1's normal buy rules. While RISK_OFF persists, the sleeve is
re-evaluated monthly and switched only if a different candidate leads by more
than 2 percentage points (hysteresis).

## 5. Tax and cost module (required, new)
- Gold ETF: not equity. Gains ≤ 12 months at slab (declared 30% + cess); > 12
  months 12.5%. No STT on buy; ETF STT on sell only.
- Nifty ETF: equity rates (20% / 12.5%), ETF STT on sell only.
- Liquid ETF: NAV flat; return arrives as dividend units taxed at slab. Model
  as cash rate 5.5% taxed at slab (declared; replace with actual dividend
  history if the feed has it).
- Zerodha delivery costs apply; DP charge per sell.

## 6. Window
E1's full window from 2007 (gold ETF listing). Development 2007–2016,
validation 2017–2021.

## 7. Sweep
| row | change |
|---|---|
| S1 | 6-month lookback |
| S2 | 12-month lookback |
| S3 | hold the sleeve through RISK_ON until it breaks its own 10-month average |
| S4 | gold only vs cash (no Nifty ETF) |
| S5 | no hysteresis |
| S6 | sell-to-cash during RISK_OFF: E1 §6's sell-to-cash variant (E1/E1b S7) is on, so the whole liquidated portfolio enters the sleeve, not just the accumulating SIP |

S6 is the row that makes this charter's question a large one rather than a
small one. Under §4 the sleeve receives only the monthly SIP while RISK_OFF
persists, which in E1b's development sweep was a mean cash share of 22.4% —
so even a sleeve that beats cash handsomely moves the headline by little.
E1 §6's sell-to-cash variant liquidates the equity book on RISK_OFF instead,
and on E1b's development window that row returned 11.24% against the base
row's 9.47%, with a 49.2% max drawdown against 54.7%. If the whole book is going
to sit somewhere during a crash, where it sits is worth far more than where
the SIP sits, and this row measures that.

It is a stability check, not an option: E1 §6 is explicit that exactly one
version of the trend rule is the main run and the sell-to-cash variant lives
in the sweep. S6 does not change which version E3's base row uses.

Two consequences to declare before the run, neither resolved here:
- The sleeve under S6 carries realised equity gains into the ETF, so §5's
  tax module sees a far larger flow and the short-term/long-term split of
  the *equity* book changes as well. The tax paid is reported per row (§8).
- The re-entry at the first RISK_ON month-end is E1's normal buy rule, which
  admits at most six names a month (E1 §5). A liquidated book therefore takes
  months to rebuild, and that lag is part of what S6 measures, not a defect
  to correct.

## 8. Reported extras
Months in each sleeve; sleeve return vs cash over RISK_OFF months only; tax
paid by the sleeve.

## 9. Not claimed
Not an asset-allocation strategy. It only acts when E1 is already RISK_OFF.
