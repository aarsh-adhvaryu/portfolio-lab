**Specified Mutual Fund (s.50AA of the 1961 Act; s.76 of the 2025 Act).** Read in the 2025 Act (G1 s.76(5)(b)): a fund that invests MORE THAN 65% in debt and money-market instruments, or a fund that invests 65% or more in units of such a fund. Units bought on or after 2023-04-01 are always short-term at slab. The definition was narrowed by the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 (before it, ANY fund with up to 35% in domestic equity was "specified", which caught international and gold funds). **Conflict found and settled:** one outlet said the new definition applies from 2025-04-01 and another that it applies from AY 2026-27; both give the same date (1 April 2025 = FY 2025-26 on). The text of the 2025 Act (the one in force) has only the narrow definition. **Consequence for the core:** an international ETF or gold fund is NOT a Specified Mutual Fund from FY 2025-26; a purchase made before 2025-04-01 of such a fund may still have been caught for FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 sales: not relevant to a new paper sub-book.

**Open points (E):** (1) one fund house's article says 24 months for listed international ETFs; the Act text (G1 s.2(101)(b)(i)) does not carve units out, so 12 months is the primary-text reading. The tax engine's `listed_nonequity` row (12 months) already encodes it. (2) Dividends from these Indian funds: IDCW is taxed at slab with 10% TDS above Rs 10,000 (S, not re-read; the core's ETFs are accumulating or low-dividend).

## 5. (d) Premium to NAV of India-listed international ETFs

**Why it happens (P for the limits' existence, S for the numbers).** Indian mutual funds may invest abroad only up to **USD 7 billion** across the industry, with a separate **USD 1 billion** for overseas ETFs, and a per-fund ceiling (USD 1 billion per mutual fund under the Master Circular of 2026-03-20 cited in scheme documents; older documents say USD 600 million, and USD 200-300 million for ETFs: the sources disagree, so the per-fund figure is **S**). The industry limit was exhausted in 2022: SEBI let funds invest again only "upto the headroom available as of Feb 1, 2022" (SEBI letter 2022-06-17 quoted in scheme documents, **S**). With no new creation of units possible, the arbitrage that holds a price near NAV stops, so the market price floats above NAV. AMFI said in June 2026 it will push again for a higher cap after the RBI's FCNR(B) deposit window (Business Today, 2026-06-26, **S**); no decision.

Motilal Oswal's scheme documents say "direct creation is allowed until further notice" in blocks of 600,000 units and an earlier AMC notice said its Nasdaq ETFs "may trade at a premium to their indicative NAV due to increased demand" (reported by Economic Times Dec 2024 and indmoney); the AMC addenda of 2022-2023 suspended lump sums into the Nasdaq 100 FoF "till further notice" (Motilal addenda, found by search; one PDF returned HTTP 500 and was not read). **No AMC suspension notice was read in full by me: E**; verify at the AMC's "notices / addenda" page and NSE circulars. **Two conflicting reports of the price-band rule:** one article says the ETF price band's base became the previous close (30-minute VWAP) from 2026-09-07; another cites a SEBI circular of 2026-06-15 moving the base to the previous day's NAV from 2027-04-01. Unsettled (**E**). Also: a 20% circuit limit on gold and silver ETFs (BSE, after a bullion fall) and a SEBI rule, from 2026-04-01, valuing gold and silver in ETF NAVs at Indian exchange spot polling (SEBI circular dated 2026-02-26, **S** via Business Today): this narrows gold NAV gaps; gold ETFs showed 5-12% over NAV in Oct 2025 and Jan 2026 (S, sensational sources).

**Rail threshold (E, his to change in `configs/core.toml [premium]`).** Hang Seng BeES, the best-behaved of the set, ran 2-5% over NAV in Sep-Oct 2026; every other international ETF ran 15% to 235%. A premium paid is lost if it closes (MONQ50 lost 36% in two sessions with NAV flat). The official NAV is struck on the underlying's earlier close, so a premium vs NAV carries about +/-1% of timing noise even in a healthy ETF. So the rail: **no new buying above 2.0% premium; from 1.0% scale the purchase down linearly to zero at 2.0%; no NAV in the last 5 days means unknown, which is treated as "do not buy"**; selling is never blocked. It is a judgement, not a measured optimum: a rule 10 study would test it.

## 6. (e) US calendar, hours, costs, route, FX and data

**Hours in IST (P: NYSE core session 9:30-16:00 New York; the offset is arithmetic on the zone).** US daylight saving ended on **2026-11-01** and starts again on **2027-03-14** (second Sunday of March; first Sunday of November: the zone database used by `conductor/us_calendar.py`). Winter (EST, UTC-5): open **20:00 IST**, close **02:30 IST the next calendar day**. Summer (EDT, UTC-4): open **19:00 IST**, close **01:30 IST the next day**. An early close (13:00 NY) lands at 23:30 IST (winter) or 22:30 IST (summer). So the US close of New York date D is processed **in the IST morning of D+1**; a Friday New York session closes early on Saturday IST. Holidays: N1 (matches the repo file).

**Costs a retail investor in India pays via GIFT City (S: Zerodha's guide read 2026-10-27, not re-opened; X1 and X2 P).**
Brokerage 0.10% each way (minimum USD 0.01, maximum USD 10) plus 18% IGST on the brokerage; IFSCA turnover fee 0.005% on both sides; SEC Section 31 fee USD 20.60 per million of sales = **0.00206%** on sells only (**P**, X1, from 2026-04-04); FINRA TAF USD 0.000195 per share, at most USD 9.79, on sells only (**P** for the schedule, X2; paused 1 October to 31 December 2026 per FINRA's billing FAQ, S); currency conversion by the bank at 0.4% to 1.4% above the RBI reference rate (S, a range); no STT, GST on trades or stamp duty in the IFSC (S, Finnovate June 2026). TCS 20% above Rs 10 lakh a year (S). The sub-book uses these as its parameter table `us/costs.py`, each row labelled.

**Route for his January-February 2027 real money (E: nothing is decided, and that is Phase C, not built).** Resident individual, under LRS (R2: USD 250,000 a year; funds not used must come back in 180 days), through a **GIFT City (IFSC) broker account**: Zerodha IFSC (INX Global Access, actual US shares, fractional, per Zerodha's guide), and other brokers named by Finnovate (S: Kotak, HDFC, ICICI Securities IFSC, DSP's GIFT City fund). Two product families: (1) US shares and ETFs via a Global Access Provider (actual shares; Schedule FA; 25% dividend withholding; the tax of section 2 and 3); (2) NSE IFSC "unsponsored depository receipts" on about 50 US large caps (also foreign assets). **GIFT City funds regulated by the IFSCA do not count against SEBI's USD 7 billion cap** (S, DSIJ/Vested). What can be verified today: the LRS limit (P), the tax law above (P/S), the IFSC wording in s.196(3), s.198(4) (P). What cannot: the brokerage schedule and the FX markup (set by the provider; re-read the live schedule), and the platform's onboarding rules.

**FX.** The RBI reference rate is now computed by **FBIL** (P, R1). The archive with an Excel download is on the RBI site (R3, interactive). FBIL's methodology: spot trades in a one-hour window around noon Mumbai; published each business day (S: one forum post says 13:30 IST; unverified). The sub-book values and trades at: (1) an `fx_rates` row `USDINR_FBIL` if one was entered (from the RBI archive file); else (2) the market USD/INR daily close from `data/global/FX_USDINR.parquet` (Yahoo `INR=X`, current to the last session); else (3) FRED DEXINUS (`fx_rates` pair `USDINR`, lags about a week). The source is stored with every rate and printed with every figure. For TAX the SBI TTBR of the previous month-end is needed (S), approximated by the same series at that date and labelled **E** (the SBI TTBR is typically lower than the mid-market rate by about 0.3% to 1%: E, check against SBI's published TTBR for a sample date).

**Free US data already in this repo.** `tools/backfill_us.py` and `data/us_prices.py` fetch Yahoo daily bars (open, high, low, close split-adjusted, adjusted close with dividends, volume, dividend per share) for SPY, QQQ, the eleven SPDR sector funds, the S&P 500 members (a list of today's constituents, so a stock-level test would carry survivorship bias: the learner tests the ETFs only) and his US names, to `data/us/raw/*.parquet` and a panel `data/us/prices.parquet` (4.4 million rows, current to 2026-11-06 for SPY, 2026-10-27 for most names; collected on 2026-10-27). Splits: Yahoo's `close` is split-adjusted and `adj_close` also dividend-adjusted. The panel's key is `ZZ`+10-digit SEC CIK when the ticker is in SEC's `company_tickers.json` (`data/sec_edgar.py`, needs a contact User-Agent), else `US:SYMBOL` for a fund with no CIK. CLAUDE.md rule 3: a US instrument uses its real ISIN when a statement shows one, else the pseudo-ISIN `ZZ` + the 10-digit CIK (12 characters), mapped in `instrument_alias` from a date: `ledger/foreign.py` (`pseudo_isin`, `add_alias`, `resolve`). Yahoo is not an official source (E). A free official alternative for fundamentals is SEC EDGAR (already used).
